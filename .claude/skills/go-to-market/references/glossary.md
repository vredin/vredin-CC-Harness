# Glossary — methodology terms used in this report

Quick definitions for anyone new to the Jobs To Be Done vocabulary that runs through the product method. The report points here once near the top; treat it as a lookup, not a chapter to read straight through.

- **Job** — the progress a person is actually after: where they stand today (call it the starting state) versus the result they want to reach (the target state), so that they can advance some larger goal. It is a unit of *motivation*, not a feature and not a product. A clean way to phrase it: *"When {circumstance + trigger}, I want to {desired result} measured by {success criteria}, so that I can {higher-level result}."*
- **Core Job** — the top-level Job that *your product carries out completely*. This is the rung your product occupies.
- **Aspiration Job** — the bigger life or business outcome sitting one rung above the Core Job. It holds the customer's deeper motivation; your product feeds into it but never delivers it on its own.
- **Sibling Job** — a Job on the same rung as your Core Job that your product *does not* handle — someone else does it: a rival, a partner, or the customer themselves. This is usually the richest vein for growth ideas.
- **Sub-job** — a fine-grained step one rung below the Core Job, where the moment-to-moment experience actually happens.
- **Job Map** — the diagram showing how all of these Jobs connect for a given segment. The rungs are defined relative to *how far your product reaches*, not by any fixed scale.
- **Success criteria** — the specific, checkable thresholds the customer uses to decide the outcome was "good enough" (for instance, "warning at least 30 minutes ahead" or "ships same day"). This is value, written as a spec.
- **Value** — less mental energy spent to finish a Job (a better result for less time, money, effort, or worry), judged against whatever the customer's brain expected going in.
- **Aha Moment** — the welcome surprise when your product handles the Job *better than the customer braced for* — proof that value actually landed.
- **Problem** — the unwelcome surprise when a product falls *short* of the customer's expected criteria. A Problem always sits downstream of some Solution that was hired for a Job and is now underdelivering — it is never the root cause by itself.
- **Solution** — whatever the customer leans on to get a Job done: a product, a hack, paying a person, or simply doing nothing. Trading one Solution for another also trades the underlying bundle of Jobs.
- **Segment** — a set of people working **comparable Core Jobs against comparable success criteria**. It is not an age bracket or a vertical — those are, at best, loose correlates.
- **Switchable demand** — the slice of a segment that is *dissatisfied and ready to move* (their Problem is what pushes them). A large segment that is content with what it already uses is not a market you can sell into.
- **Segment Map** — the full set of distinct Job-based segments in a market, annotated with each one's size, spending power, and how easy it is to reach, so you can decide where to play.
- **Selection screen** — the five checks this report runs each segment through: (1) clearly perceptible added value, (2) demand we are able to create, (3) the margin we need per paying customer, (4) money-size multiplied by willingness to switch, and (5) the absence of any fatal risk (legal blocks or impossible technology).
- **Choice Activators** — the specific facts that, once they reach the customer's mind, tilt the decision your way: a better option exists; here is exactly how it wins; here is the product and the very first step; the worries about adopting it are already covered; and the pain of the current alternative is genuine.
- **Barrier** — a hard, factual obstacle that blocks a customer from adopting a new solution at all (a missing integration, an unavailable country, an unmet compliance requirement). This is work on reality, not on the message.
- **Delivery Chain** — the Job Map stretched across a timeline: the real order of steps the customer moves through, marking where the Aha Moment ought to fire and where the experience tends to fall apart.
- **Positioning** — the bridge connecting the Core Job your product performs to the Aspiration Job the customer actually cares about.
- **RAT (Riskiest Assumption Test)** — write down the beliefs the idea rests on (market → segment + Jobs → value → unit economics → channels), rank them by how fatal each would be if false and how cheaply it can be tested, then spend first on evidence aimed at the deadliest ones. The point is to *cheaply kill or redirect* the idea, not to reassure yourself.
- **Pivot** — a change to the set of risky assumptions — frequently a move toward a different Aspiration-Job market where your current capabilities (tech, people, capital) happen to fit more naturally.
- **TAM / SAM / SOM** — Total / Serviceable / Obtainable market. Read in order: every dollar attached to the Aspiration Job; the share you're actually positioned to reach given your geographies and chosen segments; and the slice you could plausibly capture over the coming year or two.
