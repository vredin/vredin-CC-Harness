# Glossary — method terms used in this report

Short, plain-language definitions for anyone meeting the product method's
vocabulary for the first time. The report points here once near the top; treat
it as a lookup table, not something to read straight through.

- **Job** — the result a person is actually trying to reach: where they stand
  today (State A), the outcome they're after (State B), and the larger purpose
  that outcome serves. It is a unit of *motivation*, not a feature and not a
  product. A clean way to phrase it: *"When {situation + trigger}, I want to
  {outcome} judged by {success criteria}, so that {higher-level outcome}."*
- **Core Job** — the highest-level Job that your product carries out *in full*.
  This is the rung your product actually stands on.
- **Aspiration Job** — the broader life or business outcome sitting one level
  above the Core Job. It holds most of the customer's motivation; your product
  helps move it forward but never completes it on its own.
- **Sibling Job** — a Job at the same altitude as your Core Job that your
  product does *not* handle — it's covered by a rival, a partner, or the
  customer themselves. This is usually the richest seam for growth ideas.
- **Sub-job** — a small step sitting one level beneath the Core Job, where the
  moment-to-moment lived experience plays out.
- **Job Map** — the picture of how all of these Jobs connect for a given
  segment. The "levels" are defined relative to *your* product's reach, so they
  shift depending on what you build, not on any fixed scale.
- **Success criteria** — the specific, checkable standards the customer uses to
  decide the outcome turned out "good enough" (for instance, "report ready
  before the Monday standup" or "costs less than a tank of fuel"). This is value
  written down as a spec.
- **Value** — how much cognitive energy the customer's brain saves in getting a
  Job done — a better result for less time, money, effort, or worry — always
  measured against what their brain expected to spend.
- **Aha Moment** — the agreeable jolt of surprise when the product does the Job
  *better than the customer was bracing for*. It's the cue that value actually
  landed.
- **Problem** — the disagreeable surprise when a product comes in *under* the
  criteria the customer expected. A Problem always trails behind some Solution
  that was hired for a Job and fell short — it is a symptom, never the root.
- **Solution** — whatever the customer leans on to get a Job done: a product, a
  manual workaround, paying a person, or simply doing nothing. Trading one
  Solution for another means trading the whole bundle of Jobs underneath it.
- **Segment** — a cluster of people working on **similar Core Jobs with similar
  success criteria**. It is not an age bracket or an industry label; those, at
  best, are loose correlates that ride alongside the real grouping.
- **Switchable demand** — the slice of a segment that is both *dissatisfied and
  ready to move* (the Problem is what sets them in motion). A large segment that
  is content with what it already uses is not a market you can sell into.
- **Segment Map** — the full set of Job-defined segments in a market, each
  tagged with its size, spending power, and how reachable it is, used to decide
  where you'll compete.
- **Selection screen** — the five checks this report runs on every segment:
  (1) value the customer can plainly feel, (2) demand you're able to create,
  (3) the margin you'd earn per paying customer, (4) size in money multiplied by
  willingness to switch, and (5) absence of any deal-ending risk (legal walls,
  technically impossible asks).
- **Choice Activators** — the specific pieces of information that, once they
  land in the customer's mind, tilt the decision toward you: a better path
  exists; here's the yardstick that proves it's better; here's the product and
  the very first step to take; the worries you have are already covered; and the
  flaws in your current option are genuine.
- **Barrier** — a hard, factual condition that blocks a customer from adopting a
  new solution at all (a connector that doesn't exist yet, a country you don't
  ship to, a certification you lack). This is work on reality, not on wording.
- **Delivery Chain** — the Job Map stretched out along a timeline: the real
  order of steps the customer actually walks through, marking where the Aha
  Moment ought to fire and where the experience tends to fall apart.
- **Positioning** — the bridge you draw between the Core Job your product
  performs and the Aspiration Job the customer truly cares about.
- **RAT (Riskiest Assumption Test)** — naming the beliefs the whole idea rests
  on (market → segment + Jobs → value → unit economics → channels), ordering
  them by how fatal each would be if false and how cheaply it can be checked,
  then spending the least to gather evidence against the most dangerous one
  first. The point is to *kill the idea or pivot* on the cheap, not to collect
  reassurance.
- **Pivot** — a swap in your set of risky assumptions — frequently a jump to a
  different Aspiration-Job market that suits your existing assets (technology,
  people, capital) more naturally.
- **TAM / SAM / SOM** — Total, Serviceable, and Obtainable market: the entire
  pool of money tied to the Aspiration Job, the portion you can actually serve
  given your geography and segments, and the portion you can realistically win
  inside the next year or two.
