# Glossary — methodology terms used in this report

Short, plain-language entries for anyone meeting the product method and its Jobs To Be Done vocabulary for the first time. The report points here once near the top; treat it as a lookup, not a chapter to read straight through.

- **Job** — the real progress a person is reaching for: where they stand today (State A), the result they want next (State B), and the larger aim that result serves. It is a unit of *motivation*, never a feature or a product. Write it as *"When {situation + trigger}, I want to {desired outcome} judged by {success criteria}, so that {higher-level outcome}."*
- **Core Job** — the highest-level Job that your product carries out *in full*. This is the rung your product actually occupies.
- **Aspiration Job** — the broader life or business result sitting one rung above the Core Job. It holds the customer's underlying motivation; your product feeds into it but never completes it on its own.
- **Sibling Job** — a Job on the same rung as your Core Job that your product does *not* handle (a rival tool, a partner, or the customer themselves handles it). This is the richest seam for growth ideas.
- **Sub-job** — a small step one rung beneath the Core Job, the level where moment-to-moment usage actually happens.
- **Job Map** — the layout showing how all of these Jobs connect for a given segment. The rungs are defined relative to *your own* product's reach, so they shift depending on what you build, rather than being fixed.
- **Success criteria** — the concrete, checkable standards the customer uses to decide the outcome was reached "well enough" (for instance, "warning at least 30 minutes ahead" or "total cost below 50 euros"). They spell out what value means here.
- **Value** — how much cognitive energy the customer's brain saves in finishing a Job: a better result for less time, money, effort, or worry, always weighed against what the brain expected to spend.
- **Aha Moment** — the agreeable jolt of surprise when your product handles the Job *better than the customer was bracing for*. It is the tell that value actually registered.
- **Problem** — the disagreeable surprise when a product lands *under* the customer's expected criteria. A Problem always trails behind some Solution that was hired for a Job and then fell short — it is a symptom, not an origin.
- **Solution** — whatever the customer leans on to finish a Job: a product, an improvised hack, paying a person, or simply doing nothing at all. Trading one Solution for another also trades the bundle of Jobs underneath it.
- **Segment** — a set of people pursuing **comparable Core Jobs with comparable success criteria**. It is not an age bracket or a vertical; those, at best, only loosely track the real grouping.
- **Switchable demand** — the slice of a segment that is both *dissatisfied and ready to move* (their Problem is what triggers the switch). A large segment content with what it already uses is not a market you can sell into.
- **Segment Map** — the full set of separate Job-based segments in a market, annotated with each one's size, spending power, and how reachable it is, used to decide where to play.
- **Selection screen** — the five checks this report runs against every segment: (1) value the customer can plainly notice, (2) demand we are able to create, (3) the margin we can earn per paying customer, (4) size in money multiplied by willingness to switch, and (5) the absence of a fatal risk (legal blocker or impossible technology).
- **Choice Activators** — the specific bits of information that, once they land in the customer's mind, tilt the decision toward you: that a better path exists; that it wins on these particular criteria; that here is the product and the very first step; that the worries about adopting it are already taken care of; and that the downsides of the alternative are genuine.
- **Barrier** — a hard, factual condition that flatly prevents a customer from adopting a new Solution (a connector that does not exist, a country that is not covered, a certification that is missing). It is work on reality, not on the message.
- **Delivery Chain** — the Job Map stretched out along a timeline: the real order of steps the customer actually moves through, marking where the Aha Moment ought to fire and where the experience tends to fall apart.
- **Positioning** — the bridge you draw between the Core Job your product performs and the Aspiration Job the customer truly cares about.
- **RAT (Riskiest Assumption Test)** — write down the beliefs the idea is staked on — that a market exists, that there is a reachable segment with the right Jobs, that you can create value for it, that the per-customer economics hold up, and that viable channels exist — then rank them by how fatal each would be if false and how cheaply it can be checked, and spend first on evidence that could disprove the deadliest ones. The point is to *kill or pivot* for little money, not to reassure yourself.
- **Pivot** — a shift in the bundle of risky assumptions, frequently a move to a different Aspiration-Job market that suits the same assets — technology, people, capital — more naturally.
- **TAM / SAM / SOM** — Total, Serviceable, and Obtainable market: the entire pool of money tied to the Aspiration Job, the portion you can actually serve given your geography and segments, and the portion you can credibly win inside the next year or two.
