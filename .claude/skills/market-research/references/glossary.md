# Glossary — methodology terms used in this report

Short, plain-language definitions for anyone meeting the product method for the first time. The report points here once near the top; treat it as a lookup, not a chapter to read straight through.

- **Job** — the thing a person is actually trying to accomplish: where they stand today (State A) and the result they are reaching for (State B), all in service of some larger outcome. It is not a feature and not a product — it is a unit of *motivation*. Write it as *"When {situation + trigger}, I want to {outcome} measured by {success criteria}, so that I can {higher-level outcome}."*
- **Core Job** — the highest-level Job *that your product carries out in full*. This is the rung your product sits on.
- **Aspiration Job** — the broader life or business result that sits one level above the Core Job. It holds the customer's real motivation; your product helps move it forward but never completes it on its own.
- **Sibling Job** — a Job on the same level as your Core Job that your product does *not* handle (a rival, a partner, or the customer themselves handles it). This is usually the richest well of expansion ideas.
- **Sub-job** — a small step that sits one level beneath the Core Job, where the moment-to-moment lived experience happens.
- **Job Map** — the diagram showing how all of these Jobs connect for a given segment. The levels are defined relative to *how far your product reaches*, so they shift from product to product rather than being fixed.
- **Success criteria** — the concrete, measurable yardsticks a customer uses to decide the outcome was good enough (for example, "a reminder that arrives the day before," or "no more than 30 minutes of setup"). This is value, written as a specification.
- **Value** — how much less effort the customer's brain has to spend to finish a Job (a stronger result for less time, money, work, or worry), always judged against what the brain expected going in.
- **Aha Moment** — the welcome surprise a customer feels when your product does the Job *better than they assumed it would* — the sign that value actually registered.
- **Problem** — the unwelcome surprise that shows up when a product comes in *under* the customer's expected criteria. A Problem always sits downstream of some Solution that was hired for a Job and then fell short — it is a symptom, never the root.
- **Solution** — whatever the customer leans on to get a Job done: a product, a manual workaround, paying another person, or simply leaving it undone. Trading one Solution for another means trading the whole bundle of Jobs underneath it.
- **Segment** — a set of people working on **comparable Core Jobs with comparable success criteria**. It is not a demographic slice or an industry vertical — at best those things merely correlate with it.
- **Switchable demand** — the portion of a segment that is *dissatisfied and ready to move* (a Problem is what sets them in motion). A large segment that is content with what it already uses is not a market you can sell into.
- **Segment Map** — the full set of distinct Job-based segments inside a market, annotated with their size, spending power, and how easy each is to reach, used to decide where to play.
- **Selection screen** — the five checks this report runs on every candidate segment: (1) value the customer would clearly notice, (2) demand we are able to create, (3) the margin we would earn per paying customer, (4) size measured in money multiplied by willingness to switch, and (5) the absence of any fatal risk (legal walls or technology that cannot be built).
- **Choice Activators** — the specific facts that, once they land in a customer's mind, tilt the decision your way (a better option exists; here is the dimension on which it wins; here is the product and the very first step to take; the worries you have about it are already covered; the downsides of the alternative are genuine).
- **Barrier** — a hard, objective condition that blocks a customer from adopting a new Solution at all (a connector that does not exist yet, a country that is not served, a certification that is missing). This is work on reality, not on the message.
- **Delivery Chain** — the Job Map redrawn along a timeline: the real sequence a customer moves through, marking where the Aha Moment ought to land and where the experience tends to fall apart.
- **Positioning** — the bridge between the Core Job your product performs and the Aspiration Job the customer actually cares about.
- **RAT (Riskiest Assumption Test)** — writing down the beliefs an idea is standing on (market → segment and Jobs → value → unit economics → channels), sorting them by how fatal each would be if it turned out false and how cheaply it can be checked, then spending first to gather evidence against the most dangerous ones. The point is to *cheaply kill or redirect* the idea, not to reassure yourself it works.
- **Pivot** — a deliberate change to the bundle of risky assumptions — frequently a jump to a different Aspiration-Job market that suits the same assets (technology, team, capital) more naturally.
- **TAM / SAM / SOM** — Total / Serviceable / Obtainable market: the entire pool of money tied to the Aspiration Job, the share you could serve given your geography and chosen segments, and the share you could plausibly win within the next year or two.
